# Golden Multi-Hop Questions for Astrid's Research Corpus

These 8 questions were synthesized from the 21 file-level probe findings to test whether "Ask your data" can answer questions that require combining facts across multiple ingested files, rather than looking up a single number in a single document.

## 1. Everyday Multitaskers vs. Loungewear concept-test engagement

**Hunkemöller's segmentation defines 'Everyday Multitaskers' as women 30-45 who balance work, family and personal life while valuing both comfort and style. In the Loungewear Innovations concept test, which collections did the 30-44 age group actually favor most, and does that match the segment's stated profile?**

- Hop type: customer-segment definition cross-checked against a concept-test outcome for the corresponding age band
- Source files: 1. Presentation Customer Segments 2026.pptx; 2. HKM_Brandbook.pdf; 1a. Results - Concept test - Loungewear innovations 19-2-2026.pptx
- Reasoning path: Pull the "Everyday Multitaskers" (30-45) definition from both the Customer Segments deck's executive summary and the Brandbook's audience chapter (comfort+style balance while juggling work/family/personal life). Then check the Loungewear concept test's age-segment breakdown, which shows the 30-44 group favors Second Skin Collection and Thermo Essentials, driven by fit and functional warmth rather than softness (unlike 18-29, who lead engagement at 3.94-4.10 stars on softness/comfort). Compare the segment's "balance" framing to the concept test's functional/fit-driven preference.
- Expected answer: The 30-44 (Everyday Multitaskers) group favors Second Skin Collection and Thermo Essentials, chosen for fit and functional warmth rather than softness — consistent with a segment defined around practical balance rather than the trend-driven, softness-first preference shown by 18-29 (Young & Expressive), who score highest overall (3.94-4.10 stars).

## 2. Comfort as the top purchase driver vs. actual Super Comfort Bra performance by cup size

**Comfort and good fit are the #1 stated purchase drivers for bras (per the Bra Shopping Experience survey). Does the Super Comfort Bra product test show Hunkemöller actually delivering on that driver equally across cup sizes, and how does this relate to the 'Confident Classic' segment's larger cup/band sizes?**

- Hop type: stated top purchase driver checked against actual product-test performance, segmented by a customer-segment attribute
- Source files: 4a. Conversation Studio - Survey Results - The Bra Shopping Experience 10-9-2025.pdf; 2a. Results - Product test - Super Comfort bra 15-5-2026.pptx; 1. Presentation Customer Segments 2026.pptx
- Reasoning path: From 4a, comfort/fit is the top driver at 55%, ahead of quality (41%) and price (33%). From 2a, average satisfaction by cup size drops from 4.2 (cup A) to 3.3 (cup D), with L/XL wearers citing side gaping, lower support and a less flattering shape as main complaints. From the Customer Segments deck, Confident Classic (45-60) is explicitly described as having larger cup/band sizes. Combine to see whether the segment most defined by larger sizes is the one least well served on the industry's #1 driver.
- Expected answer: No — comfort/fit is the top driver (55%) per the Bra Shopping survey, but the Super Comfort Bra test shows satisfaction falling from 4.2 (cup A/S-M) to 3.3 (cup D/L-XL), with larger-size wearers reporting gaping and support problems. Since Confident Classic (45-60) is defined partly by larger cup/band sizes, this segment is disproportionately underserved on the very driver that matters most to bra buyers.

## 3. Germany's cross-quarter trend across three Brand & Campaign trackers

**How has Hunkemöller's performance in the German market trended across the quarterly Brand & Campaign trackers from SWIM Q2 2025 through Spring Q1 2026?**

- Hop type: cross-quarter trend built from three separate tracker decks spanning three quarters
- Source files: 8a. Report - Brand & Campaign tracker - SWIM Q2 2025.pptx; 7a. Report - Brand & Campaign tracker - FEWIY+Cashmere Q3 2025.pptx; 5a. Report - Brand & Campaign tracker - Spring Q1 2026.pptx
- Reasoning path: From 8a (Q2'25), Germany swimwear awareness ranks only #7 (vs #1 in NL), and the 18-34 vs 35-54 trend line shows total awareness falling 83% (Q2'23) to 77% (Q2'25) and purchase 13% to 7% for the 35-54 base. From 7a (Q3'25), DE lingerie category average spend is €114 vs a €121 benchmark, with purchase frequency at parity (2.7 vs 2.7). From 5a (Q1'26), DE has a similar share of category buyers vs benchmark but lower purchase frequency and lower average spend than benchmark. Chain the three data points to characterize a persistent DE monetization gap even as awareness metrics fluctuate.
- Expected answer: Germany shows a persistent underperformance pattern across three consecutive quarters — low relative brand awareness/purchase incidence in Q2'25 (awareness rank #7, historical decline 83%→77%, purchase 13%→7%), spend still below benchmark in Q3'25 (€114 vs €121, frequency at parity), and by Q1'26 DE still lags on purchase frequency and average spend despite comparable category-buyer share — indicating a persistent monetization/frequency gap in Germany that the tracker consistently detects quarter over quarter.

## 4. "Comfort" as the winning brand-promise domain vs. nightwear friction data and the Brandbook

**The German brand-promise research found 'Comfort' was the winning positioning domain. Does this match the actual top consumer frictions reported in the customer-segments nightwear survey, and how does the Brandbook's brand promise reflect this alignment?**

- Hop type: brand-strategy research finding validated against separate consumer pain-point survey data and brand documentation
- Source files: 3a. Results - Research Brand promise DE 8-8-2025.pptx; 1. Presentation Customer Segments 2026.pptx; 2. HKM_Brandbook.pdf
- Reasoning path: From 3a, among 703 German bra buyers, comfort and good fit are the top purchase drivers across all age groups, and "Comfort" wins as the positioning domain over "Indulgence." From the Customer Segments deck's nightwear friction survey (slide 20), the top complaint is nightwear twisting/bunching while sleeping (33% 18-29, 24% 30-44, 25% 45-60), with fit mismatch a close second (~28-30% across ages) — both are literal comfort/fit problems. From the Brandbook, the brand promise chapter centers on soft stretch fabrics and comfort ("affordable luxury," tagline about feeling good). Combine to check whether the strategic choice of "Comfort" as the winning domain is grounded in real, measured consumer friction.
- Expected answer: Yes, they align — "Comfort" won the DE brand-promise domain test because comfort/fit are the top purchase drivers for German buyers, and this is corroborated by the segments deck's nightwear friction data, where the top complaints (twisting/bunching ~24-33%, fit mismatch ~28-30%) are comfort/fit issues across every age band; the Brandbook's brand promise chapter (soft stretch fabrics, comfort-first positioning) formalizes this same insight into Hunkemöller's core brand identity.

## 5. TVC creative profiles mapped onto customer segments

**The Super Soft World TVC pre-test found the 'Nicola' video polarizes by age (stronger with 18-29, weaker with 45-60) while 'Monica' is more balanced and slightly stronger with 45-60. How do these two video profiles map onto Hunkemöller's Young & Expressive and Confident Classic customer segments?**

- Hop type: ad-testing age-skew data linked to customer-segment personas to validate a creative targeting recommendation
- Source files: 3a. Results - Pre-test TVC - Super Soft World 7-4-2026.pptx; 1. Presentation Customer Segments 2026.pptx
- Reasoning path: From the TVC pre-test, Nicola scores 8.0 (18-29), 7.3 (30-44), 6.9 (45-60) — polarizing by age; Monica scores 7.6, 7.4, 7.0 — more consistent and relatively stronger with 45-60; the strategic recommendation for the 45-60 group is to prioritize realistic/credible representation over stylized emotional storytelling. From the Customer Segments deck, Young & Expressive (18-30) is spontaneous/trend-driven/price-sensitive, while Confident Classic (45-60) is comfort/fit/price-quality focused. Match Nicola's stronger pull with 18-29 to the Young & Expressive persona, and Monica's steadier/older-friendly profile to Confident Classic's practical orientation.
- Expected answer: Nicola's video, which scores highest with 18-29 (8.0) and drops most with age, aligns with the Young & Expressive segment's trend-driven, spontaneous profile; Monica's more consistent scores (7.6-7.0) and relative strength with 45-60 (7.0 vs Nicola's 6.9) align with Confident Classic's practicality and quality focus — supporting the pre-test's recommendation to use realistic, functional representation (Monica-style) rather than stylized storytelling (Nicola-style) when targeting the 45-60 segment.

## 6. Shared satisfaction pattern across two product tests

**Comparing the Loungewear concept test's age-based engagement scores with the Super Comfort Bra product test's cup-size-based satisfaction scores, what shared pattern emerges about which customers Hunkemöller currently serves best versus worst?**

- Hop type: parallel satisfaction patterns across two different product-test studies, keyed to two different but related customer attributes (age vs cup size)
- Source files: 1a. Results - Concept test - Loungewear innovations 19-2-2026.pptx; 2a. Results - Product test - Super Comfort bra 15-5-2026.pptx
- Reasoning path: From 1a, women 18-29 show the strongest engagement across all loungewear innovations (3.94-4.10 stars), well above the collection averages (3.66-3.83). From 2a, satisfaction with the Super Comfort Bra is highest for cup A (4.2) and cup B (4.1) — sizes typically worn by S/M customers — and falls to 3.3 for cup D (L/XL), with specific complaints (gaping, lower support) concentrated in larger sizes. Line up the two studies to see whether younger/smaller-size customers are consistently the best-served group across product categories.
- Expected answer: Both studies point the same direction — younger and smaller-size customers report the highest satisfaction (18-29 loungewear engagement 3.94-4.10; cup A/B bra satisfaction 4.2/4.1), while larger sizes fare worse (cup D at 3.3, with gaping/support complaints), suggesting a recurring pattern where Hunkemöller's product development currently favors younger, smaller-bodied customers over the older, larger-cup Confident Classic segment across at least two separate product categories (loungewear and bras).

## 8. Converging creative recommendations for older German consumers

**Two independent German-market studies — the brand-promise concept test and the Super Soft World TVC pre-test — each produced messaging/creative recommendations. Do their recommendations for reaching older German consumers point in the same direction?**

- Hop type: converging strategic recommendations synthesized from two unrelated studies (verbal messaging guidance vs video-creative guidance) targeting the same demographic
- Source files: 3a. Results - Research Brand promise DE 8-8-2025.pptx; 3a. Results - Pre-test TVC - Super Soft World 7-4-2026.pptx
- Reasoning path: From the brand-promise DE deck, messaging guidance recommends avoiding negative-association words ("pinching"/"sliding") and notes German audiences, especially older ones, prefer formal "Sie" address and less English phrasing. From the TVC pre-test deck, the strategic advice for resonating with women 45-60 is to prioritize realistic/credible representation and functional product communication over stylized or abstract emotional storytelling. Compare both recommendations, which independently target older German consumers, to see if they share an underlying creative principle.
- Expected answer: Yes — both studies converge on the same principle for older German consumers: keep communication grounded, credible and unembellished. The brand-promise research recommends formal "Sie" address, avoiding English loanwords and negative-connotation words for this audience, while the TVC pre-test recommends realistic, functional representation over stylized storytelling for the 45-60 segment specifically — two independently-sourced studies both point toward plain, credible, non-gimmicky creative execution for older German customers.
